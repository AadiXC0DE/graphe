**A long job now finishes.** Three separate things each believed they decided when a turn ended and what happened next, and none of them knew the other two existed: an add-on holding every settle hostage while it drained its own background work, a second-opinion gate that turned "finish" into "report", and the window advancing your checklist by guessing at every reply boundary. One of them alone stops a twelve-step list. All three together is why a job that should have run all afternoon stopped after two steps and wrote a summary.

There is one thing now that decides whether Graphe sends itself the next message — for a list still unticked, a goal not yet met, work finishing on the board, an add-on asking for a turn, or an error worth picking up — and it says out loud what it decided and why. Every way it stops says so. There is a Stop beside it.

**The checklist is the model's, and nobody else touches it.** A reply that read three files and wrote a paragraph used to tick a step. A failing test used to fail one. A reply that stopped to ask the advisor ticked the step it had not finished, and then carried on past it — so work was skipped and the list still finished. None of that can happen now: the model says which step moved, by number, and the app never guesses.

The list is also complete. A twenty-step plan gave you twelve steps and a caveat; now it gives you twenty. A finished list stays on screen saying so instead of deleting itself the moment it reads as done — a list that finished by mistake used to vanish with nothing to resume from. And every conversation has its own, so a second tab no longer advances the first one's.

**What Graphe stands for travels with every turn.** The checklist used to be appended to the message you typed. So a steer carried it, a retry after a rate limit did not, and neither did anything after the conversation was tidied up — exactly the turns where a long job forgets it had a list. It is in the system prompt now, on every call, and the prompt has a budget you can see.

**Add-ons cannot hold or hijack a turn.** Every add-on gets a line in Add-ons saying what it will actually do — "starts turns on its own · runs work in the background · changes the system prompt" — worked out by asking the add-on, not from a list of names. One that starts work of its own keeps its tools and loses its hooks, and there is a switch behind the model chip if you want it back. Anything that takes too long at the end of a turn is let go of rather than left to hold the app.

**Your `.env` is never committed.** Graphe saves a version at the end of every turn, and in a repository with no ignore rule for it that included your credentials. It never does now, whatever `.gitignore` says — and one already in your history is reported rather than quietly touched. Opening your Desktop, your home folder or iCloud Drive is refused with a sentence instead of turning it into a repository and committing everything in it.

**When something goes wrong, there is something to send.** A rotating log with secrets masked before they reach it, an error boundary around every view, one calm card, and Copy diagnostics — the version, this machine, the add-ons, the last lines of the log and why the last job stopped. No conversations and no keys.

Also in this release:

- Escape puts the composer back to Send in one render, and no longer advances the list, applies the checkout or takes screenshots for work nobody finished.
- A rate limit is waited out even when you have a message queued behind the run.
- A model that reports no cost no longer leaves the tab spinning for the rest of the sitting.
- Landing a conversation is one commit with your message by default, and your `pre-commit` hook runs on it.
- Settings says which build this is, what the app is holding on disk, and offers to clear the finished work — never a copy holding work you have not taken in.
- Graphe has its own menu. ⌘R no longer reloads the window mid-run.
- The Figma token lives in your keychain, so it works when you launch from the Dock.
- A typed draft survives a restart.
- Long conversations open and stream without the window doing nothing else.
- Every helper, server and language server this app starts is written down, and quitting takes them with it.
- Research offers a plan card to approve rather than writing a checklist and starting on it.
