**Add-ons work out of the box.** They were on a shelf you could install from and then quietly half-disabled: an add-on kept its tools and lost its hooks, which is not caution. An add-on whose tool starts the work and whose hook delivers the result launches and then never answers. That is what "the subagent produced no output" was on the surface.

Underneath it was something else. `process.execPath` inside Graphe is Electron, not node, so an add-on spawning the runtime it is running under (the obvious way to start a fresh agent) got a second Electron application: a GPU process, a network service, a window that never appears. From a terminal that child answers in three seconds. From inside Graphe it hung indefinitely with nothing on stdout, forever, and reported it as a model that had said nothing.

Both are fixed. Add-ons are on by default, they run whole in a conversation, and a child of this app can actually be a child of this app. What made the hooks dangerous was two things deciding when a turn begins. That is handled where it belongs now: a turn an add-on asks for is one reason among the app's own, counted against the same budget and named out loud, and a lifecycle handler that stops answering is let go of rather than allowed to hold the settle.

**Make it yours.** Five colour presets were the whole of the appearance, and a preset is somebody else's taste. Now it is an accent, a tone, a contrast, a corner radius, a density, a motion setting and two fonts, and every surface, border and piece of text is derived from them in OKLCH and *measured* before it lands. There is no combination that produces something nobody can read; the test sweeps nine accents across three tones and two grounds and asserts every pair clears 4.5:1, and 7:1 on high. The preview is the real stylesheet on real surfaces, and hovering a choice shows it before pressing commits it.

For anyone who wants a token the builder does not offer, there is `graphe.css` under the app's own folder, loaded last so it wins.

**It is faster, and the numbers are real ones.**

- The agent runtime is 810 ms of import, and it used to be paid by whoever opened the first project, so the slowest thing the app does was the first thing anybody asked of it. It now starts while the window is already up.
- The shell sent a message across the wire for every token. Sixty tokens a second was sixty trips and sixty synchronous handlers in the window. It sends a frame's worth now, and the order a conversation is read in is unchanged, which is the property that is actually tested, across two interleaved conversations.
- A long reply was re-lexed from the top on every token, which is the same work as reading it sixty times a second. A token now re-reads the block it landed in: a 200-block document reads three.
- Eight views are reached by a press and none is on screen at launch, so none of them is in the bundle you wait for. The main chunk went from 989 KB to 687 KB.
- And there are marks now where there was one: launch, first paint, project open, first token and settle, printed in the diagnostics, because a target nobody measures is a wish.

**Naming a file is one keystroke.** `@` offered skills and nothing else, so naming a file meant typing its path correctly from memory, and a path typed by hand is one the Guard has to refuse when it is wrong, which is the app arguing with you about spelling. It offers files, folders and skills as one ranked list, and what lands in the message is a real path.

**The palette and the keyboard agreed to agree.** They were two lists written separately, and they had drifted: the palette printed ⌘⇧N for a new conversation while the keyboard bound ⌘T to that and ⌘⇧N to something else, and it advertised ⌘⇧F for a key nothing answered at all. One registry holds every action and its chord now, and both read it: the keyboard asks the registry what a press means rather than comparing keys of its own, so a chord that moves in the list moves under your hands. And you can move one. Press the row in Settings, then press the keys; Backspace clears it, and two actions landing on one chord say so where it happens rather than being found later by pressing it and getting the wrong one.

**Five reasons to carry on, and only two of them were wired.** The app decides for itself when to send a message nobody typed, and it had five reasons to: a checklist with steps left, a goal not met, background work that landed, an add-on asking, a run that fell over. Two of those reached the thing that decides.

Goal Mode did not run at all. The window wrote the goal down with no conversation on it and the authority read it with one, so they were two files that were never the same file, and a goal ran one round and rested. Background work landing had two senders and the wrong one was live: the window watched the board go quiet and prompted the conversation itself, which skipped the round budget, and did nothing at all when no window was open. An add-on's own turn was invisible: not counted, not named, and not in "why did it stop".

All of it is one path now. Who asked for a piece of background work is written down and survives a restart. A conversation with nothing running is settled there and then, because no settle of its own is coming. An add-on that asks past the budget is stopped rather than told afterwards. And a question answered with a click is answered: only typing used to clear the hold, so the first Yes ended the automatic rounds for the rest of a twelve-step job.

**Finished work waits to be looked at.** A conversation's work was carried into your folder on every settle, so files under you changed while you were reading something else and the first you heard of a clash was a sentence in a thread. It arrives in a queue now and nothing moves until you say so: per file, Accept, Keep mine or Take theirs; then Land as one commit with your message, or keeping every version, or a pull request. Whoever liked the old way has a live mirror switch on the card.

A clash is a screen rather than a sentence and an editor. Mine, theirs, keep both, or hand the two to the conversation. A file nothing can make sense of is offered no decision at all and comes back byte for byte, because a parser that guesses at half a marker deletes somebody's afternoon.

**A diff you can read.** The side-by-side view had its whole model and no screen. Split and unified over the same pairing, the changed words marked inside a changed pair, syntax colour and those marks cut on the same characters, and only the rows on screen drawn, so a twenty thousand line change is a few dozen elements. Two presses on any hunk ask the conversation about it by file and line.

**The panel's three git bands are one.** Where the branch is, what is uncommitted as `+734 −7` across so many files, and the two presses that move either. Reading three stacked bands to learn one thing was the whole of what was wrong there. The goal has a band of its own with the four numbers that were on four screens on one line, and a step that is done is struck through.

**Settings is seven pages you can search**, and the model chip is three rows. It held seven things behind a control whose job is "which model answers the next message"; it holds the model, how long it thinks, and who advises. The advisor's two gates and how much of an add-on runs moved to pages of their own, and an add-on's row is a three-way drawn from its own capability card rather than from its name.

**A long conversation opens.** Reopening one folded its whole history into the page. It draws its tail and offers the rest, and ⌘F finds a word anywhere in it, not only in what is drawn. Tabs can be arranged, because a row nobody can arrange is a row nobody can learn, and conversations older than a month fold under one heading.

**Also in this release**

- The prompt size is out of the model chip. A number with no unit, no scale and nothing to press, dropped between two switches, is furniture rather than information. It is in the diagnostics, where somebody is already asking why. What an add-on's tools cost every call is on the add-on's own row, where it can be acted on.
- The prompt has a budget and it is applied where each piece is read rather than after the whole thing is assembled, so a forty kilobyte `AGENTS.md` reaches the model at eight with a pointer to the rest.
- Every helper, server and language server this app starts is written down: subagent children, connected tool servers, the language server and the browser. Quitting ends all of them.
- Nothing anybody reads carries an em dash any more, and the build fails on the next one.
- Terminals and browser tabs still have their models and their tests and not their screens. That is the next release, and this note would rather say so than imply otherwise.
