**Computer use has a screen.** Graphe could already work the other applications on your Mac: press a button in Figma, read a workbook, drive a browser. There was nowhere to see that, hold it to a few sites, or take it back off. Settings > Computer use is that place. Any App switches desktop control off altogether. The built-in browser has a list of the sites it may reach, and holding it to a few is enforced twice: the Guard refuses an address that is not on the list, and the browser it drives is started with the same list. Excel works with no add-in to install, because the workbook is read from the file itself and the app is driven by its named buttons. Always-allowed apps skip the per-turn question for the apps you name, and only that question: passwords, keys, disk tools, and anything else the Guard refuses still refuse, listed or not, on every rung including "get on with it".

Locked use decides whether work nobody is sitting in front of, a piece on the board or a scheduled one, may touch your applications while you are elsewhere. Off, it refuses and says so, and the conversation in front of you is unaffected. Graphe never unlocks a Mac by itself, and the screen says so rather than leaving you to find out.

Nothing you could do before this release stops working because of it. Any App ships on, because the per-press question was always the gate, and a screen is not a reason to take a capability away from people who already had it.

**The app stopped talking to itself out loud.** When a checklist has steps left, or a goal is not met, Graphe sends the next round itself rather than making you type "continue" eleven times. Those rounds went out as ordinary messages and came back, when you reopened the conversation, looking like paragraphs you had typed. They were offered in the list of moments you could go back to, as well. They are the app's own words. They are left out of both now, the same way the end-of-sitting note-keeping already was, and so is a turn an add-on asked for on its own.

**Two buttons that did nothing are gone.** "What does this do?" on an add-on row asked the model a question behind the panel and then wrote "Asked. The answer is in the conversation." under the row, which is the app describing its own plumbing. "Show me how it starts" folded out values nobody had gone looking for. Both are off the Add more screen.

**Review uses the window.** It was held to 1400 pixels wide, and a diff is read across as well as down, so on a wide screen a third of it sat empty while the code wrapped. The list of what is waiting keeps its narrow column; everything else is the change.

**The before-and-after strip is off for now.** It turned up under turns that changed nothing anybody could see, which teaches you to stop opening the ones that matter. The pictures it takes still feed the version timeline and the handoff summary. Only the card in the conversation is gone, until it can tell the difference.
